AI-AGENTI · mind map

The method on one surface

Sixteen chapters of principles, sorted by the question they answer. At the centre sits the sentence everything else follows from — each branch is only its consequence in a different part of the system.

Language: Čeština English

The map

Read from the centre outwards. The left side answers “what is it made of” and “how does it fit together”, the right side “how do you talk to it”, “who keeps it in check” and “how does it live in production”.

Foundation

  • The model recognises, it does not execute. Three jobs: intent, structure, text.
  • Ownership test: can it be written as if–then? Then it is code.
  • Free text, speech, image, human intent → model. Anything else → code.
  • Determinism is cheaper by an order of magnitude. The difference is not the model, but how much work it is handed.

chapters 1 · 2 · 16

Construction

  • Anatomy: channels → intent → main mind → fixed processes → gates → output.
  • Modules with contracts. Each has its own CLI, tests and gate.
  • Contracts are frozen before the first line of code.
  • A subagent gets a narrow brief and partial permissions — it has no way to go wrong.
  • The persona is generated from source material and corrected by hand, never written from memory.

chapters 3 · 4 · 13

core

AI recognises.Code executes.

Either the process fails and reports it, or it goes well.
There is no third possibility.

Contact with the world

  • Chat on a phone beats a form. Dictating a sentence is faster than six fields.
  • The channel is chosen to fit the situation — someone who is driving gets a voice message, not text.
  • Memory in three layers: durable in the repo, factual in the database, working in the context.
  • Factual memory does not belong in the prompt wholesale.
  • Proactivity: speak up when a question or a task follows from it. Not merely when something happened.

chapters 5 · 9 · 10

Control

  • Identity is bound to the channel, not to a name in the text.
  • Whitelist, not blacklist. Anyone not listed has no access.
  • The approval mode is set by how reversible the mistake is, not by trust in the model.
  • Foreign input never triggers an action directly.
  • Cross-check with two independent passes — for numbers, dates, amounts, names.

chapters 6 · 7 · 8

Operation

  • Silent failure is the worst outcome. An agent that says nothing looks like one that is working.
  • A kill switch that takes one action. Tested, not theoretical.
  • The log records decisions, not just results.
  • Learn from your own corrections, not from metrics. How much you correct is itself a diagnostic.
  • A prompt is never rewritten automatically. Version it, do not overwrite it.

Entry point — chapter 14

The design sheet. Filled in before the first line of code exists. Twelve sentences it all shrinks to

Chapter 16 of the principles. If you take nothing else from the map, take this.

  1. AI recognises, code executes.
  2. Either the process fails and reports it, or it goes well. The third branch must not exist.
  3. The model gets three kinds of job only: intent, structure, text.
  4. The persona is generated from sources, not written from memory.
  5. Identity is bound to the channel, not to a name.
  6. The approval mode is set by how reversible the mistake is, not by trust in the model.
  7. Where accuracy matters, verify with two independent passes.
  8. Chat on a phone beats a form.
  9. An agent that stays quiet when it crashes is worse than no agent.
  10. Learn from your own corrections, not from metrics. And never automatically.
  11. Modules with contracts, then gates, then integration.
  12. Fill in the design sheet before you write the first line.
