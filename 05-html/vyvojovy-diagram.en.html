From an idea to a deployed agent

Nine phases, each closed by a gate. A gate is a list of conditions — until all of them hold, the next phase does not begin and the work goes back. Each gate catches one class of mistake that the following phase would hit anyway, only more expensively.

The chart

A solid arrow is progress forward; the dashed one on the left is the return to the same phase when the gate does not pass. The highlighted phases are not skipped even for the smallest agent.

The critical path, and where it usually goes wrong

The critical path is F0 → F1 → F3 → F5. These four carry the risk of the whole project; the rest is catch-up work that can be done later.

The most common mistake is skipping F1 and F3 — the model gets started before a process exists. From then on you are debugging two unknowns at once and cannot tell whether the fault is in the prompt or in the backbone.

Three are never skipped: the real sample in F1, the two outcomes in F3 and the kill switch in F6. Their absence does not show during development — it shows in the damage.

What a small agent may trim

The specification is a maximum, not a minimum. For a simple agent built for yourself, F0, F1, F3 and F6 stay mandatory. The rest can be shortened — deliberately, not by omission.
